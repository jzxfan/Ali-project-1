How I got into it

I got introduced to RC drift by one of my close friends. He had just gotten into it and wanted a buddy to drive with, so he kept telling me to get one until I finally caved. I ended up pulling the trigger on a Ready to Run Yokomo RD2 from my local hobby store.

The first car

Right away I noticed the RTR didn't drive very good. It was really difficult to drift and that was mostly because of the low end electronics it came with. I was told the first things I should replace were the servo and the gyro. After I swapped those out for some nicer ones the car immediately drove way better and it was sooo much easier to drift. That was the moment I was hooked.

The whole idea of being able to tinker on these 1/10 scale cars and get them to drift exactly how you want, and match your exact driving style, had me wanting more upgrades and doing more and more research.

Why RC instead of a real drift car

Honestly I get all of the enjoyment out of my RC cars that I get out of my real car, if not more. They are so easy to work on, and if something breaks it doesn't keep you from getting back home like a real drift car would. It's a cheap fix and 15 minutes of sitting in a chair with a hex screwdriver and you're back out there.

Upgrades I'd tell a beginner to do first

If you're starting out with an RTR like I did, this is the order I would go in. Counting down to the most important one:

  1. Tires: Different surfaces grip differently, so it's worth having a couple sets for wherever you drive the most.
  2. Motor and ESC: A nicer brushless motor and speed control makes the throttle way smoother, which helps a lot when you're trying to hold a drift.
  3. Servo and gyro: This is the big one. It's what took my car from frustrating to actually fun, so do this first.

From two friends to a whole crew

What went from 1 car quickly went to 3. The more I got into the hobby and researched all the science and physics behind upgrading these chassis, the more I started bringing my friends into it too. We started out as just two of us and now we have like 7 or 8 friends who have picked up RC drift cars.

Going to the RC track on the weekend to drive with friends and get 1/10 scale drift clips is honestly one of my favorite things to do. Everyone helps each other out with tuning and parts, and it's just a good time.
